# Use a three.js shader for the landing Blob field

The landing page's background is the Blob field: three soft colour blobs that cruise slowly across the whole viewport behind the headline, bounce off its edges, and overlap like layered spray paint. It is rendered by one full-screen quad and one fragment shader: each blob is a soft disc in blob-local space (solid inside `r(1 - feather)`, gone at `r`), a screen-fixed hash gives every grain cell its own threshold so the fade breaks into scattered dots whose density follows the falloff while the core stays solid, two slowly rotating plane waves give each outline a small organic wobble, and the blobs are composited back to front with a plain "over" so a later blob simply paints on top of an earlier one. Blob centres come from a tiny CPU drift (`src/lib/landingPage/blobDrift.js`): each blob starts at its seed's home spot, travels in a straight line at a constant fraction of the viewport per second, and is reflected off an edge when the dense part of its paint (`EDGE × r`) meets it, retaining 70% of its extra kick. Blob-to-blob contact uses a smaller `0.5 × r` solid core with a soft spring-damper (~100 ms response, damping ratio 0.6); the cores compress and push apart without billiard-ball impulses while their feathered rims can still overlap into a soft metaball-like neck. Grabbing connects a core to the pointer through a stiff, critically damped hold spring (~80 ms response), so it follows promptly while remaining part of the same contact simulation and pushes free blobs aside. `blobDrift.check.js` proves the motion, collision, bounds, and speed-cap invariants. An earlier version used a density metaball (`mix(max, sum)`) with a home-tethered random walk that floored the pair distance so two blobs formed a neck without fusing; it was rejected because the additive field bulged where two blobs approached and averaged their colours into a pale halo that read as nothing in the reference, and the floor held the blobs at exactly the neck-forming distance. The current effect comes from feather overlap around physically colliding cores, not from summing the shader fields. CSS or SVG goo filters were rejected because a filter over a full viewport re-rasterises every frame, and a pure time-driven shader cannot bounce centres off edges. As in ADR-0002, three is loaded by a dynamic import inside an attachment, so the landing's first paint ships no WebGL; all text and links stay in the DOM above the canvas; reduced motion freezes the drift and renders one still frame; a browser without WebGL keeps the plain paper background. This supersedes ADR-0001: the DOM card deck and project wall are gone; the landing is a typographic nav over the Blob field. While the scene is active, mouse, pen, and touch can grab dense paint through the hold spring and throw it; the released kick fades on a 0.7-second time constant, retains 70% of its extra speed at an edge, and re-aims the ambient cruise; a press that does not grab pokes nearby blobs away; one-finger pans stay with the Blob field while pinch zoom remains available; disposal removes the pointer listeners and cursor state; and reduced motion ignores all input.
