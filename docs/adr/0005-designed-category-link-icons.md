# Design each Category link icon, on one shared line-art stage

ADR-0004 copied the uikit-expt card whole, including its two icons (Columns, Cubes) and their hover spin. The icons were stand-ins; each Category link now has its own designed icon whose hover *shows* the category. The rest of ADR-0004 stands — the springing brackets, the label's black bar, the instant switch to violet ink, the react-spring port — except that the frame's faint border and the lit card's shadow are gone and the brackets show only on the lit link (they fade in as they spring out), so at rest each icon is drawn straight on the page and lit it is a card of the page's own white.

The three icons share one stage (`icons/stage.js`): a WebGL canvas per link, the reference's orthographic camera and 45°/45° turn, white boxes inked at their UV borders by the `fwidth` shader, and one ink uniform that turns violet. Each icon is a module that poses its own meshes every frame and says whether it wants another (`frame(dt, { lit, zoom, reduced })`), so the link's loop runs only while something moves or an icon is looping. The spin is gone; nothing rotates for its own sake.

- **Interactive maps** — a closed tri-fold (one cuboid) unfolds as a Z-fold chain of three panels, turning from 22° to face the camera square at 45°, the one angle where the zigzag reads as the familiar folded-map silhouette. Contours are isolines of one noise field laid in paper space across all three panels (so they carry on over the folds), a pixel thinner than the edges, kept off the borders by a neatline margin, wiped in left to right rather than faded, and flowing left to right while lit.
- **Visual stories** — a thin book, nearly as wide as it is long, opens into a symmetric 124° V on a big spread (an asymmetric spread read as a laptop), and five cubes of different sizes pop up through the gutter one after another, then melt into and out of one another in two rows on a fixed 7 s script: apart, two pairs, one blob, new pairs, apart. The cubes are raymarched in the same canvas: rounded boxes joined by a polynomial smooth union and cut at page level, writing their own depth so the pages hide what is below them. Where the surface is still one cube's own it is inked at the cube's edges by exact distance (UV derivatives break up along a raymarched edge); where cubes have melted only the silhouette is inked, so necks stay soft.
- **Web tools** — a browser window reflows: two cards push out of the screen, the window glides from desktop to phone, and at the breakpoint the cards snap from a row into a column, then it breathes between the two. The container glides rigidly; only the content snaps. On its way to phone it becomes one: its corners round off and the title bar gives way to a screen in a bezel, with an island at its top and a home indicator at its foot. The window is one rounded slab sized in its vertex shader and inked by signed distance (its outline, the phone's parts, the wall's rims and, round the corners, its silhouette), so it can change width, height and corner radius every frame with round corners and hairline ink.

The icons stand half as big again as the mockup's (2.625em frames). Lit, each doubles: the brackets and a white card behind the drawing scale as vectors while the drawing zooms in the camera (so its lines stay hairlines).

The headline makes room for the doubled card instead of being covered by it (`headlineFlow.js`), the way a page makes room for a picture set into its text. The card grows from its top-left corner, so the words before it and the lines above stay put, and on its own line the words after it are pushed along by exactly as much as it widens (past the column into the margin, if the window has room). The lines below stay where they are: where the card comes down over one, that line is cut round it letter by letter, as Pretext's demos flow text round shapes one line band at a time. The text runs on to a word space short of the card, splitting a word between two letters if it has to ("web t | card | ools"), and carries on past it, so a word the card lands on bursts apart and closes up again when it goes. A cut opens as the card comes down onto the line's letters and is shut again before it is back at rest.

A split word is drawn twice: the word itself, clipped to the letters before the cut, and an aria-hidden copy clipped to those after it, drawing its text as generated content so screen readers, find and copy see the sentence once. The letters stay where the browser set them, kerning and all, and a cut falls in the gap between two letters' ink. Only where the stream would run past the window's edge (a phone) does it wrap onward, between words, dissolving what jumps and forming it again; there a lit link's name may break between its two words, its black bar running on across the break. Everything moves by transform, clip-path and opacity in the same frame as the card, and at rest the headline is the browser's own layout; Pretext checks it once, and if its line breaks ever disagree with the browser's, the flow switches off and the card magnifies over the sentence.

Rejected for making room: parting the lines below to let the card in (shipped briefly: calm, but it moved the whole page on every hover, where cutting the line round the card leaves it where it is); text wrapping round the card like a float (tried first: the card sat mid-line, the next links were too wide for the stretch beside it, so the sentence tore into holes and a narrow column, most words changed line, every in-between frame was a different layout, and a link pushed onto another line slid back out from under the pointer); CSS alone (a real float or growing inline box reflows the page every frame); per-word springs toward the new layout (words pass through one another mid-flight).

Each has its own verb (unfold, open, reflow) and its own kind of motion (hinges and a flowing field, organic goo, rigid glide and snap), so the three read as a set rather than one idea three times. Open states ease down in scale to stay inside the lit brackets; reduced motion shows one chosen still of the lit state.

Rejected: modelling the icons in Blender and loading glTF (the edge ink needs box UVs, and it would add files to fetch for shapes made of a few boxes); marching-cubes metaballs (no crisp cube edges while apart); CSS 3D (see ADR-0004). For web tools, a design panel also weighed a window whose slider knobs get adjusted, drag handles steering a data cube, and a stack of tiles dealt into a sliding-puzzle grid; the reflow won because it is the one that is unmistakably *web*.
