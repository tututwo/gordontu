# Design each Category link icon, on one shared line-art stage

ADR-0004 copied the uikit-expt card whole, including its two icons (Columns, Cubes) and their hover spin. The icons were stand-ins; each Category link now has its own designed icon whose hover *shows* the category. The rest of ADR-0004 stands: the frame, the springing brackets, the label's black bar, the instant switch to violet ink, the react-spring port.

The three icons share one stage (`icons/stage.js`): a WebGL canvas per link, the reference's orthographic camera and 45°/45° turn, white boxes inked at their UV borders by the `fwidth` shader, and one ink uniform that turns violet. Each icon is a module that poses its own meshes every frame and says whether it wants another (`frame(dt, { lit, zoom, reduced })`), so the link's loop runs only while something moves or an icon is looping. The spin is gone; nothing rotates for its own sake.

- **Interactive maps** — a closed tri-fold (one cuboid) unfolds as a Z-fold chain of three panels, turning from 22° to face the camera square at 45°, the one angle where the zigzag reads as the familiar folded-map silhouette. Contours are isolines of one noise field laid in paper space across all three panels (so they carry on over the folds), a pixel thinner than the edges, kept off the borders by a neatline margin, wiped in left to right rather than faded, and flowing left to right while lit.
- **Visual stories** — a book of two halves opens into a symmetric 124° V (an asymmetric spread read as a laptop), and three cubes push up through the gutter and melt into and out of one another on a fixed 5 s script. The cubes are raymarched in the same canvas: rounded boxes joined by a polynomial smooth union and cut at page level, writing their own depth so the pages hide what is below them. Where the surface is still one cube's own it is inked at the cube's edges by exact distance (UV derivatives break up along a raymarched edge); where cubes have melted only the silhouette is inked, so necks stay soft.
- **Web tools** — a browser window reflows: two cards push out of the screen, the window glides from desktop to phone, and at the breakpoint the cards snap from a row into a column, then it breathes between the two. The container glides rigidly; only the content snaps.

The icons stand half as big again as the mockup's (2.625em frames). Lit, each doubles: the brackets and a white card behind them scale as vectors while the drawing zooms in the camera (so its lines stay hairlines).

The headline makes room for the doubled card instead of being covered by it (`headlineFlow.js`). The card grows from its top-left corner, so everything before it stays put; [Pretext](https://github.com/chenglou/pretext) lays the rest of the sentence out again around it every frame, the way text wraps a floated image, and the words move there by transform only, so the page never reflows. The label follows its card: beside it, reaching past the column if the window has room, or on the next line clear of it. The bio and everything below slide down on a spring if the sentence needs another line. At rest the headline is the browser's own layout; Pretext positions are only used as offsets between two of its own layouts, and if its rest line breaks ever disagree with the browser's, the flow switches off and the card magnifies over the sentence as before. Pretext is fetched after the fonts, off the first paint.

Rejected for making room: CSS alone (a real float or a growing inline box reflows the page every frame, and the words jump between lines rather than glide); per-word springs toward the new layout (words pass through one another mid-flight).

Each has its own verb (unfold, open, reflow) and its own kind of motion (hinges and a flowing field, organic goo, rigid glide and snap), so the three read as a set rather than one idea three times. Open states ease down in scale to stay inside the lit brackets; reduced motion shows one chosen still of the lit state.

Rejected: modelling the icons in Blender and loading glTF (the edge ink needs box UVs, and it would add files to fetch for shapes made of a few boxes); marching-cubes metaballs (no crisp cube edges while apart); CSS 3D (see ADR-0004). For web tools, a design panel also weighed a window whose slider knobs get adjusted, drag handles steering a data cube, and a stack of tiles dealt into a sliding-puzzle grid; the reflow won because it is the one that is unmistakably *web*.
