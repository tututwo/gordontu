# GSAP for timed motion; the site's own physics and canvases stay on requestAnimationFrame

Everything on the site that plays over a set time is a GSAP tween or timeline: the Bio revision and its scrambles (a GSAP tween that swaps each letter for random ones of about its width; ScrambleTextPlugin, tried first, draws from one set of letters, which in a proportional face cuts letters at word edges and runs words together), the avatar's nudge, the book icon's goo script and the web tools icon's squeeze script, and the Postcard gallery's fly to the centre and back and its staggered reveal. Where a loop owns a canvas, it seeks a paused tween or timeline by its own frame time (the icons by their clocks, the gallery by its frame), so each canvas keeps one clock and a gallery opened in a background tab has finished its reveal when you come to it, as before. Hand-written clamps and ranges in files that use GSAP are `gsap.utils`; `svelte/easing` is gone. The gallery imports `gsap/gsap-core`, as its tweens are all on plain objects, which keeps the CSS plugin off a postcard's deep link (about 20 KB gz there instead of 27); the landing already loaded GSAP.

What GSAP does not do stays our own. GSAP has no spring that keeps its velocity when its target moves: `quickTo` and every tween restart their ease from rest (measured: +83 px/s turned into −624 px/s on a retarget), so a quick hover in and out of a Category link would stop dead and set off again where it now turns round, and the web tools window would trail its script by up to 13 px. So `spring.js` (ADR-0004), the Postcard gallery's coast, rubber band and release-speed fit (`pan.js`), the flip's damped spring (`wallMotion.js`) and svelte/motion's Springs on the avatar all stay. Library controls were prototyped against them and measured: three's MapControls has no momentum or rubber edge, lets a tap's jitter nudge the plane 4 px, freezes a pinch when a third finger lands, and steps 7 px per arrow key; GSAP's Draggable with InertiaPlugin coasts a slow flick a third as far on a fixed quartic curve, cannot pinch, and adds 15.6 KB gz; the flip on Draggable's snap runs 12–15° off the spring mid-turn, kicks 8% at release, and flips a spinning card again when it is tapped.

These loops run on `requestAnimationFrame` through `frameLoop.js`, not on GSAP's ticker. The ticker was tried: it dispatches at most every 4 ms or so (a frame in six is skipped at 240 Hz), after it has slept the first frame of a hover can come a frame late, and it ran out of step with svelte/motion's loop (the held glasses led the page by a frame while it scrolled), which took two workarounds to mend.

CSS keeps what it does well: hover colours, fades, the rail tooltip's glide, the index caret, the startle strokes, the typing caret and the no-JS failsafe run on the compositor and turn round natively when a hover reverses half way, which a tween would replay at full length.

The same audit swapped in what the platform and three already do: `loader.loadAsync`, three's own context-loss handling and transparent clear, DOMMatrix for the glasses' affine, GLSL's `smoothstep` in the avatar's shader, the popover API for the project index and help (Escape and a tap elsewhere close them, and only one is open at a time), and `svelte/reactivity/window`'s `devicePixelRatio` to redraw the gallery sharp after a zoom or a move to another screen. The open postcard now flies from the card's live spot at the card's own depth and is sized in whole device pixels, so a close lands without a one-frame shimmer and the back's type maps 1:1.

Rejected: `gsap.matchMedia` for reduced motion (it reverts and replays animations when the setting flips; svelte/motion's `prefersReducedMotion` is read when something happens); THREE.MathUtils for the one-line lerps and smoothsteps (0.6 KB gz to rename them); EdgesGeometry with LineSegments2, OutlinePass, MarchingCubes, RoundedBoxGeometry and three for the avatar morph (ADR-0005's reasons measured to still hold: uneven line weight on thin faces, soft rims, per-frame rebuilds, WebGL2 only).
