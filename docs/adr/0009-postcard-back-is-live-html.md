# The postcard's back is live HTML, turned with the table's card every frame

An open postcard's back holds everything about its Project and the way to it (CONTEXT.md): its words and the Open project button. It used to be a canvas texture drawn with the 2D API onto the card's back face, with a transparent real link laid over the drawn "Open project ↗" (ADR-0002, amended 2026-09-24). That kept the flip in three.js, but every word was a picture: a button's hover, press and focus had to be faked on the overlay, and the drawing's layout code (wrapping, shrinking, leaving things off) duplicated what CSS does.

Now the back is ordinary HTML in the site's type (`PostcardGallery.svelte`'s `.back`), and three draws only blank white paper on the card's back. With every frame it draws, the scene reports where the card is as CSS sees it (`scene.js`'s `HeroFrame`): a translate to the card's centre, its turn, its tilt and its scale, and a perspective equal to the camera's distance in CSS px. The gallery writes these straight onto the back's style in the same frame, so the words move with the paper through the flip, the fly-in and the flight home. A point on the table is `zoom` CSS px per world unit from the window's centre, where the camera looks, so a perspective of `cameraZ × zoom` px about the window's centre projects the back exactly as the camera projects the paper. Three's y points up and CSS's down, which reverses the tilt's sign; the turn keeps its sign. Squarely on its back and at rest, the transform is a plain translation, so the words rasterise as sharply as the rest of the page. Filmed against a dark table with the HTML back outlined in red, the outline stays on the paper's edges throughout the turn.

A back can be taller than its picture. A landscape picture on a phone makes a card about 190 px tall, too short for a Message and a button, so the back takes its words' height (at most the window's). As the card turns past edge-on, the paper grows toward that height, following how far the back faces you (`−cos` of the turn), and the HTML back is clipped to the paper's current height. The caption under the card moves down with it. Because the growth follows the flip's spring, it is as interruptible as the flip. On laptops and tablets the words fit the card and nothing grows.

Rejected: a snapdom picture of the HTML back as the texture, swapped for the live HTML at rest. The two could differ by a sub-pixel at the swap, and snapdom's first picture of a page is slow, which would hold up the automatic turn on arrival. A CSS-only flip of an HTML card after the WebGL card lands would mean handing the card from one renderer to the other at both ends of the flight. A back that scrolls inside a card-sized box, as a Wallet pass does, showed a phone about three lines of the Message.
