# The Peel: canvas-ui's sheet, fed a picture of the page by snapdom

Going to or from a Postcard gallery, the page peels off over the next (CONTEXT.md), as canvas-ui's Peel does (canvasui.dev/docs/components/peel). `src/lib/peel.js` ports its WebGL sheet from `PeelVanilla.ts`: a 96×96 grid bent round a cylinder in the vertex shader, projected in perspective, shaded along the curl and lined along its edge (dark on a white page, as its "auto" shine is), with its bow, bulge and perspective defaults. Canvas UI's pointer drives how far the sheet peels; here a GSAP tween does, seeked by a frameLoop (ADR-0006), rolling the fold from the edge to past the far side in 0.9 s. The curl keeps one radius, 0.3 of the window's shorter side, instead of growing with the peel, so the edge lifts first and the roll then crosses the page, as paper turns. The back of the sheet shows the page's print mirrored, as canvas-ui's does; a paper-white back was tried and looked calmer but less like the reference. The licence is MIT with the Commons Clause, which allows the port as part of this site; its notice heads `peel.js`.

Canvas UI draws the live page into its canvas with the html-in-canvas API, which only Chrome has, behind a flag or an origin-trial token. A View Transition shows the old page as the browser's own snapshot but gives no pixels to curl. So snapdom draws the page (DOM to SVG to canvas): the viewport, over the page's white. Measured on the site in headless Chrome at 1440×900 ×2: snapdom took 120 ms for its first picture of the about page and 30–60 ms after, against modern-screenshot's 230 and 200 ms; both drew the WebGL canvases blank. Those canvases (the gallery's table, the Category link icons, the avatar) now keep their drawing buffers (`preserveDrawingBuffer`), which made the picture match the page to 0.1% of its pixels, all sub-pixel text edges. A gallery's table is drawn under snapdom's picture straight from the GPU, with its CSS drop shadow as the 2D canvas's own shadow (Safari's 2D canvas has no `filter`), because snapdom encoded it as a PNG first: four fifths of the picture's time. One picture is taken a couple of seconds after the page is up, a frame's work at a time, as snapdom's first is three to four times as slow as the rest (it embeds the fonts and learns the default styles). SvelteKit's asset links are root-relative now (`paths.relative: false`): snapdom finds stylesheets by their `link.href`, which re-resolves a relative link against the current URL, so after the app moved from `/about` to `/maps/<slug>` it fetched `/maps/_app/…` and got a 404.

SvelteKit's `onNavigate` waits while the picture is taken and laid over the page as the sheet at rest, then changes the page under it, and the sheet peels. From click to sheet, in production: 50–100 ms here, 150–320 ms with the CPU slowed four times. A picture that takes over a second is dropped and the page simply changes; without WebGL 2 too.

Rejected: holding the old page still in a View Transition while snapdom draws it (so nothing on it moves in the meantime): the browser suppresses animation frames during a transition's update, and snapdom waits on frames in several places (a WebGL canvas that is still blank, its font warm-up, Safari's image decoding), each then running to its timeout, up to a second. A CSS page curl on the View Transition's snapshot (no print on the curl, and none of canvas-ui's look). html-in-canvas behind an origin-trial token (Chrome only). A peel between the landing's tabs, and when a postcard opens or closes (those change the URL inside one page).
