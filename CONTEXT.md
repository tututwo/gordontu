# Portfolio

This context describes how Gordon's creative and technical work is presented to visitors.

## Language

**Project**:
A published piece of Gordon's creative or technical work, with a title, image, Message, tools, category, Client, and date, and a web page of its own unless the Project is just its image.
_Avoid_: Post, moment, photo

**Message**:
What a Project's postcard says on its back, in Gordon's words: one to three sentences on what the Project is and what a visitor can do with it. A Project may not have one yet.
_Avoid_: Description, blurb, summary, caption

**Client**:
Whoever a Project was made for, by name (World Bank, Yale University); two or more share one line. A Project nobody commissioned is Self-initiated.
_Avoid_: Customer, partner, organisation type (Nonprofit)

**Featured Project**:
A Project Gordon has picked to show on the landing's projects tab. The rest are only in the Postcard galleries.
_Avoid_: Highlight, selected work, pick

**Project card**:
How a Featured Project appears on the landing's projects tab: its image, then its title with its Client beneath, and its year at the right edge. A Project with a video plays it over the image, from the start, while a mouse is on the card, and the image comes back when the mouse leaves; a finger, or reduced motion, keeps the image.
_Avoid_: Row, tile, profile pic (for the image: that is Gordon's avatar)

**Landing**:
The home page and its four tabs, on plain white. A headline ("I'm Gordon", his avatar, then what he makes) names the three Project categories as Category links; below it come the social links (X, LinkedIn, GitHub), a rule, and a quiet text nav of tabs (the open one underlined; the underline glides to the tab you open): about, projects, writing, contact, each with its own URL (`/about`, `/projects`, `/writing`, `/contact`). On a phone it all sits closer, the rule gone, so the first screen reaches the bio's scrambled last line. Switching tabs changes only the panel below the nav; the headline stays put. The home URL opens on about, the grey bio that carries the Bio revision and ends in the Afterword. projects shows the Featured Projects as Project cards, newest first, each opening its postcard in its category's Postcard gallery, then a View all link to the All projects Postcard gallery; writing is the Blog, a placeholder line until the first post; contact is the Contact page.
_Avoid_: Hero, landing nav, card deck, menu

**Intro**:
How the Landing opens at most once per 24 hours in each browser: blank but for the avatar, which draws itself where it sits in the headline, about seven seconds in all. One pen draws the drawing's own lines, one after another, each seen growing from nothing to its end (hair, fringe, face and ears, brows, eyes and mouth, collar, shoulders), then the glasses (a rim, the bridge, the other rim), and a broad brush paints its colours in under the lines, sweeping back and forth down the hair and then the face. The pen (a pen tool's nib) and then the brush (its tuft in the paint it lays) are drawn where the hand is, on each line's growing end and moving between them. Then it is simply the avatar; he blinks once, and everything else shows around him, its text scrambling in: all of it turns at once into light look-alike letters (as in the Afterword), and every line decodes at the same time, in step: one front sweeps left to right down all of them at once, in about a second and a half. Nothing moves meanwhile: each word keeps its own width. A tap, a key or a scroll hurries the drawing to its end. The browser records it when it starts, so repeat visits and reloads within 24 hours show the page immediately, even if the drawing was interrupted. It never plays on a move within the site, with reduced motion, or when browser storage is unavailable.
_Avoid_: Loading screen, splash, preloader

**Contact page**:
The landing's contact tab: a line under the tabs inviting a project, a question or a hello (it goes straight to Gordon's inbox, and he reads every one), then a short, scripted chat with Gordon (his lines are written, nothing answers live) that asks one thing at a time, casually: the visitor's name, then their project (what the data is, who it's for, any deadline or budget), then where to write back. Gordon's lines are white hairline boxes with his face (the landing's avatar drawing) beside each run of them; the visitor's are the grey wash, flush right. Each of Gordon's lines comes in thirty wordings, plain, friendly and to the point, and every visit draws one of each at random, so the chat doesn't read the same twice. A name some of Gordon's people go by sets off a Name joke in place of his "nice to meet you". The chat has no box round it: it is the column itself, and its lines scroll on their own, fading at its edges, while the page scrolls on around it as ever. It takes the rest of the screen under the intro, never less than 28rem nor more than 36rem: a tablet upright or a large window shows it whole on arrival, a laptop a short scroll down; on a phone, where the headline fills the first screen, it is nearly a screen tall once scrolled to, and a phone held sideways fits it whole. The visitor types every answer in one composer at its foot, as in Muse: the grey wash, from the left edge of Gordon's lines to the column's right edge; an answer tapped to change it opens there, until the message is sent. The chat ends on the letter as Gordon will get it (from, the message) and a Send button; anything typed then joins the letter as a P.S. After sending comes "Sent" with the address he will reply to, and a 30-minute call on his calendar for anyone who can't wait. Below the chat is the way round it, set like the "Worked with" row after it: a "Prefer email?" eyebrow, the address unboxed in the type and grey of Gordon's lines with a copy button that is only its icon right beside it, and a helper line saying he usually replies within 1–2 days; then the names he has worked with (VISA from the bio, then the Projects' Clients). The page sends the letter itself, without the visitor leaving the page or opening an email app, to Gordon's inbox, where replying answers the visitor's address. His inbox is where messages are kept; the site keeps none.
_Avoid_: Inquiry, lead, contact form (for the whole page), chatbot

**Name joke**:
What Gordon says, on the Contact page, instead of "nice to meet you" when the visitor's name is one of his people's: Winston (sworn at for costing him web traffic instead of texting), Heather (his partner), Karla ("Hai ya~~"), Jen, Jenn or Jennifer (greeted as Jenn, as if already on Slack together), Gordon (Gordon too, Tu), Wendy (who could just WeChat him), Luisa, Ziqi or Qiqi (asked why they never do dinner anymore), and Zaba or Zababa (asked out for a drink at Agnes' place again). Any word of the name counts, in any case, but only a whole word: Jenkins is not a Jen. Each joke allows that the visitor may just share the name, and has five wordings, one drawn per visit; the chat then goes on to the project as usual. A name with two jokes gets the first in that order.
_Avoid_: Easter egg, special greeting

**Category link**:
One of the three links set into the landing headline, one per Project category: a line-drawn 3D icon, followed by the category's name, leading to that category's Postcard gallery. On hover the icon doubles in size as a white card and the sentence makes room like a page round a picture (the rest of its line moves along, and the lines below stay put while it cuts through them, splitting a word between its letters where it lands), corner brackets spring out around it and it plays its own reveal (in black line, lit or not), and a black bar wipes across the name. Leaving reverses it. Keyboard focus plays it the same way. A finger cannot hover, so on touch the first tap plays it, a second tap follows the link, and a tap anywhere else reverses it. Each icon is on the page from its first paint, as a still of itself, and comes alive once its three.js is in. The icons: Interactive maps is a folded map that unfolds into a zigzag with contour lines flowing across it; Visual stories is a book that opens and lets five cubes rise out of it and melt into one another; Web tools is a browser window that narrows into a phone while its cards reflow.
_Avoid_: Button, pill, chip, card

**Bio revision**:
The about bio's second sentence, a self-correction left on the page: "I use ~~d3.js, three.js+GLSL/TSL, React&Svelte, QGIS, Blender etc..~~ Claude Code, Codex & Jev across my toolkit to design and build interactive 2D&3D experiences." The old tool list is struck through by a pen-like scratch, one stroke through the middle of its letters, cut at its line breaks, sinking a little as it goes and running on a little past the list's end (without script, a plain strikethrough). Styled like a suggested edit, but in grey only. It is simply there, not played.
_Avoid_: Typo, draft, tracked change

**Afterword**:
The about bio's last line, the afterthought "Alright, you really wanna know ha: I practice Chen- and Yang-style tai chi, play acoustic guitar, and I’m learning tango with my retired neighbor.", is simply there as a cipher (each letter swapped for another of about its width, capitals and punctuation kept, set in the bio's own weight but light grey, spaced to fill each word's box): only the avatar's Glasses read it. While it is a secret, each time a pointer comes over the paragraph, or a finger taps it (a scroll that starts on it does not count), it scrambles, and under a pointer reads dark until the pointer leaves; the avatar glances down at it, wondering (three question marks by his hair), and a mouse becomes a pair of round glasses with a question mark, drawn in the avatar's line, that draw themselves on. While it is decoded (read with the Glasses and let go) these hints rest, and only the Glasses still read it; once it is a secret again, they are back. Once the Glasses have read it, letting go of them decodes the whole line where it stands, word by word; three minutes later, or once the page is left (hiding the browser tab, or going to another tab of the site, in which case it comes back decoded and scrambles into its cipher a moment later), it scrambles back, played, not switched. Reduced motion swaps cipher and words at once.

**Glasses**:
The avatar's glasses, which come off. Pressing the avatar lifts them off to one side, bigger, as if held up to the visitor; the head turns to follow them and startles (three strokes by the hair). Dragging carries them anywhere on the page (scrolling it on at the window's edge), and through their lenses the Afterword reads plainly, each word decoding calmly (a slow, thin flicker, then settling) the first time a lens comes over it while they are held; over those words the glasses come a quarter bigger, as if brought closer to read. Held with the glasses themselves within 45° of straight below his head (wherever the hand holding them is), they alarm him: the three strokes stand up one after another into three exclamation marks; anywhere else, they stay three strokes. While they are held, the head looks up at the pointer when it is within 75° of straight above it, and down when it is within 75° of straight below, bending into the drawings of him looking up and looking down; looking up, he does not startle. A finger carries them higher above it than a mouse does, clear of the fingertip. Let go, they spring back on and the head turns back (and if they read the scramble, it decodes). Worn, the glasses leave the head as it is.
_Avoid_: Magnifier, lens tool, X-ray

**Postcard gallery**:
A page of Projects scattered once as postcards on a bounded plane you drag around. There is one per Project category, holding that category's Projects, and one titled All projects (`/all`), holding every Project mixed together, newest first; its tool rail reaches all four. Each postcard is its Project's image in its own proportions. One postcard can be opened (it flies to the centre; picking it from the project index does the same). The front is only its picture; under the card sit just a Flip for details button and, at the far end, Close. Everything about the Project, and the way to it, is on its back, which is plain paper with words on it and nothing made to look like a postcard: its date and category, its title, its Message, its Client and tools (beside the words on a wide card, under them on a narrow one), and, for a Project with a web page of its own, Open project, the one filled button, at the foot. Its type grows with the card, from a phone's to a laptop's. A card too short for its back's words (a landscape picture on a phone) has a longer back: the paper grows to it as the card turns over, and shrinks back as it turns to the front. Dragging or clicking the back turns it over, as with the front; its button opens the Project's web page. An open postcard has its own URL, the gallery's plus the Project's slug (`/maps/<slug>`, `/all/<slug>`): opening one puts it in the address bar, closing takes it out, and arriving at it (from a Project card, a shared link, a reload) opens that postcard face up and, once it lands, turns it over to its back, since whoever arrives that way came for that Project; opened within the gallery, it stays face up. Flipping never changes the URL.
_Avoid_: Grid, wall, canvas, infinite canvas, Project page (there is none: a Project's URL is its open postcard)

**Peel**:
How the page changes on the way into or out of a Postcard gallery (canvas-ui's Peel, played by the navigation): the page you leave lifts at one edge, curls over in perspective, shaded along the curl, and rolls off the far side, and the next page is underneath. Going on (from the landing into a gallery, or to a gallery further along the rail) it lifts at its right edge, like a book's page turning to the next; going back, at its left. Switching the landing's tabs and opening or closing a postcard don't peel; nor does a phone's swipe back, which the browser animates itself; reduced motion changes the page at once.
_Avoid_: Page flip, page curl, page transition, turn

**Project category**:
One of the portfolio's canonical groupings: Interactive maps, Visual stories, or Web tools. Each has its own page (`/maps`, `/charts`, `/creative-code` — the URLs keep the earlier names Maps, Charts, Creative coding), reached from the landing page or the gallery's section switcher. A Project stores its category's `slug`, the URL form.
_Avoid_: Title tag, navigation item, filter, services
